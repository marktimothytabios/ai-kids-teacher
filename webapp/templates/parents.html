For parents

These books work best when they are a shared moment, not a lesson. A few simple habits make them much more powerful.

How to read a story

  1. Ask first. Read the question on the left page and wait. “Why do you think the sky is blue?” Every guess is a good guess.
  2. Read the answer. It is short on purpose, so you can add your own words and examples.
  3. Do Let's try! Moving, touching and pretending makes it stick.
  4. Talk about it. The green box has a question with no wrong answer.
  5. Use the Grown-up corner. It has the fuller explanation, for when your child asks “but why?”

One or two stories at a time is plenty. Little children learn best in short, happy bursts.

Printing

  • Pages are square, 8 × 8 inches (20 × 20 cm). Print at “Fit to page” on Letter or A4 paper, or choose a custom 8 × 8 inch size if your printer allows.
  • Print double-sided if you can, so each question has its answer on the back. Heavier paper (120–160 gsm) lasts longer.
  • Staple or hole-punch the sheets into a booklet, or slip them into a folder.
  • The sticker-chart page near the end is meant for {{ "colouring" if spelling == "uk" else "coloring" }} or stickers.
  • You may print as many copies as you like for your own family or classroom, but please do not share or resell the files.

Good to know

Are the answers exactly true?

They are simplified for ages 2–4, and we have tried to keep every statement correct. The Grown-up corner gives the fuller picture. If you spot a mistake, please tell us.

Are the activities safe?

They use household things and your supervision. Always follow your own judgement: hot things, water, small objects and sharp things are for grown-ups to handle.

Why no app?

Toddlers learn best from people. The books ask you to talk, move and play, so your attention, not a screen, does the teaching.

Which spelling should I pick?

Use the US / UK switch at the top of the page. The words are the same, only spellings and a few everyday words differ (for example, color or colour, store or shop).
